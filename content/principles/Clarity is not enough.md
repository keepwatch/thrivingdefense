C. Thi Nguyen is a philosopher with a wide scope of interests (he wrote a fascinating book about the philosophical implications principles of games: *[[Gaming - Agency as Art]]* ). In his paper "The Seduction of Clarity"[^1], Nguyen argues that human beings have a natural "hunger" for the feeling of understanding — that "a-ha!" moment. Anyone who does knowledge work (or enjoys puzzles) has experienced this feeling. It generally brings "a sense of closure to an investigation". It can create a problem though, because if systems give us clarity without necessarily giving us truth, we stop looking for deep, more complex answers. This is a key component of Nguyen's writing (including his book): 

> Real life is complex and, unlike in a game, clarity (of objective/goal or achievement/progress) is very difficult to achieve.

This is a powerful principle that I plan to develop fully later.

[^1]: Nguyen, C.. (2021). The Seductions of Clarity. Royal Institute of Philosophy Supplement. 10.1017/S1358246121000035.
