In 2023, I saw Sounil Yu (now [CTO at AI security startup Knostic](https://www.knostic.ai/about-us)) present at [ACoD](https://acod.io/) about the imminent transition to AI-assisted knowledge management. He presented the four-stage [DIKW pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DIKW_pyramid) describing the relationship between data, information, knowledge, and wisdom. 

![[DKIM_pyramid.png]]
[Image source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DIKW_pyramid#/media/File:DIKW_Pyramid.svg)

There's a fair amount of dispute about how to define these categories precisely, but they make intuitive sense. Consider the now-vestigial skill of "Google-fu", crafting a query to induce search engines to provide the right answers. Asking "what is the best air conditioner to buy for a NYC apartment living room" would return Zillow listings, Best Buy posts, and other SEO spam, but searching for "window A/C 6000 btu (best OR review)" would likely give useful results. However, current AI chatbots will happily infer meaning from context clues, translating "NYC 2 bedroom" to "window A/C and 250 square feet (therefore 6000 BTU)" in the background, as well as presenting a summary of results or a recommended approach. You can now receive  ==knowledge==, not just ==information==, from queries made to computers.

Sounil noted that we have major workflows and industries focused on Information: "Information Technology", "Information Security", "Information Management", "Information Engineering". What kinds of workflows happen when we replace "Information" with "Knowledge" in those descriptions?
